Getting Started with Step Builder

How to turn a recorded procedure into a step-by-step procedure your team can follow on a RealWear headset — from the recording landing in your queue to the finished procedure going live.

Application: https://realwear-troubleshooter.web.app
Use Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge. No login required.

1How the pieces fit

Step Builder is the middle of three pieces. Knowing what sits on either side of it makes everything else in this guide make sense.

This guide covers box 2 only.

2Before you start

Open https://realwear-troubleshooter.web.app in Chrome or Edge. There is no sign-in screen. The application opens on Manage Procedures, which is where you choose what to work on.

There are three ways out of this screen, and picking the right one is the whole decision:

  • Open a draft — carry on with work somebody saved to the cloud.
  • Resume editing — go back to the copy still sitting in this browser. The amber bar only appears when there is one.
  • + Create Procedure — start from nothing.
Use Chrome or Edge

In Safari and Firefox the Save As to file… option is not shown at all — the browser does not support the feature it relies on. Everything else works, including Save As to cloud…, but you lose the ability to choose where a backup file goes.

Your name

The first time you open Review Videos, the application asks for your name. It uses this to record who approved, rejected or commented on a recording.

This is a label, not a login

The application says it plainly: "Not an account — just attribution." Nobody verifies what you type, and anyone using this browser can change it. It exists so your colleagues can see who made a decision, not to control who is allowed to make one.

3Reviewing a recording

Recordings made on the headset arrive here on their own. Your job is to watch them and decide which ones are worth turning into a procedure.

Open Procedure ▾ > Review Videos….

Watching a recording

Click a row to open it.

  • The player — watch the whole thing before you decide.
  • The four decisions, left to right: Publish, Unpublish, Reject, Trash.
  • Notes — type and press Add note. Tick at current time first and the note remembers the moment you were watching, which is how you point a colleague at the exact problem.
  • The details panel: status, when it was recorded and uploaded, duration, size, which headset, who reviewed it, and the job ID.

What each decision means

DecisionWhat it means
PublishGood enough to build a procedure from. Only published recordings appear in Import from Remote, so this is what makes a recording usable.
UnpublishTake it back out of that list without judging it. Reversible.
RejectNot usable — wrong job, unusable footage. Stays on file with your name against it.
TrashQueue it for permanent deletion. It moves to the Trash tab and nothing is destroyed yet.
Every decision is signed and kept

Your name and the time are stamped on each change, and the history stays with the recording. This is recorded on the server, not in your browser, so it cannot be edited away afterwards.

Deleting a recording for good

Permanent deletion is only offered from the Trash tab — you cannot destroy a recording straight from the queue. The recording's history survives as a record that it existed and who removed it.

Permanent means permanent

The video file itself is gone. If it was the only copy of that job being done, it cannot be recovered from anywhere in this application.

4Turning a recording into steps

This is the step that saves the most time. Instead of scrubbing through the video taking screenshots by hand, the application watches it for you and proposes the steps.

Open File ▾ and choose an import.

Picking a recording

Save first if you have unsaved work

Importing wipes the procedure currently open. If you have edits worth keeping, use Save or Save As to cloud… before you import. See Saving and reopening.

While it works

It goes through a few stages — checking the video, sending it off to be analysed, then capturing a still frame for each step it found. The analysis runs in the cloud, not in your browser, so a long recording does not slow your machine down. A 90-second recording takes roughly half a minute. Leave the tab open.

What you get back

The application fills in a complete draft: a title for the whole procedure, and for every step a title, a description, an estimated duration, and a still frame from the video as the background. The canvas orientation is set from the video, so you never choose it by hand on an imported procedure.

  1. Procedure title — written from the video, sitting in the top bar. Click it and edit it freely.
  2. The step list — one card per step, in order, with + Add Step at the bottom.
  3. The canvas — the selected step, with its captured frame as the background.
  4. Step Details — everything about the selected step, from its title down to its duration.
Treat it as a first draft, not a finished job

The suggested steps are a starting point. Read every description, fix anything the application misheard, and delete steps that are not really steps. You are the one who knows the procedure.

5Editing a step

Each step is a picture with things drawn on top of it. Use annotations to point at the thing the operator is supposed to look at.

Click anything you have added to select it. The right-hand panel changes from Step Details to the properties of that item, and changes back when you click the background — so the panel always describes exactly one thing.

The small circle at the end of the callout's tail is the tip of the pointer. Drag it onto the part you are talking about and the tail follows; drag the bubble itself to move the whole thing.

The rest of the editor toolbar

ControlWhat it does
Add ▾The annotation palette — Appendix B.
Undo / RedoReverses your last change on the current step.

Hide panelCollapses the right-hand panel to give the canvas more room.

Undo works per step

Each step keeps its own undo history. Moving to another step and back does not let you undo changes you made on the first one. Get a step right before you move on.

6Working with the step list

The column down the left is the whole procedure at a glance.

  • The number follows the order of the list — reorder the steps and the numbers correct themselves.
  • The thumbnail — click it to open that step on the canvas.
  • Duplicate copies the step, annotations and all. Handy when two steps show the same equipment.

To reorder: drag a card up or down the list and drop it where you want it, or use Move Up / Move Down.
To add a blank step: use + Add Step at the bottom of the list.

7Step details

Everything the operator will read, hear and see for this one step — plus the bookkeeping that travels with it when you publish.

FieldWhat to put in it
TitleThe step's one label. It appears in the step list, on the headset, and in the PDF — there is no separate short name.
InstructionsWhat the operator does. This is the sentence the headset reads aloud, so write it as you would say it.
AlertsA safety or attention message. Pick Note, Warning or Danger and type the text. The headset shows it as a badge and speaks it before the instructions, most severe first.
ToolsOne tool name per row. The headset gathers them under TOOLS NEEDED on the procedure screen and shows them beside the step. They are never read aloud.
BackgroundChange image… replaces the step's picture; the swatches beneath set a plain colour instead, for a step that does not need a photo.
Video clipA short piece of video for this step — see below.
DurationUnder Details. How long this step takes, in your own words ("2 min"). Filled in from the video on import. The panel adds them up and shows the procedure total, which is the figure the headset displays.
Alerts are spoken, tools are not

An alert is the one thing guaranteed to reach an operator who is not looking at the screen. Save it for what actually hurts — a live circuit, a hot surface, a part that only goes on one way round. A step where every line is a Danger badge is a step nobody listens to.

Attaching a video clip

Attach a clip… under Video clip opens the chooser.

The clip plays in the headset's VIDEO panel for that step. Keep it short: the headset downloads every clip in a procedure before an operator can use it offline, so a long clip costs everybody waiting time.

Changing the background picture

Pulling a fresh frame is the fix when the imported one is blurred or caught mid-movement: pick the recording, scrub to a better moment, and take that frame instead. The dialog says it plainly — a video you choose from your own computer is only scrubbed locally, never uploaded.

8Checking your work

Before publishing, look at the procedure the way an operator will.

Click Preview in the top bar. Move through with the buttons along the bottom, and close with Close preview (Esc).

Read each step and ask: could somebody who has never done this job follow it from this picture and this sentence alone? If not, that is the step to go back and fix.

The buttons are a picture of the headset, not a working one

REMOTE ASSIST, AI ASSIST, SPLIT, VIDEO and the rest are drawn so you can see what your step will look like surrounded by them. They do nothing here. What they will do on the headset is covered by the headset's own guide.

9Publishing to the headset

Publishing sends the finished procedure to the RealWear headsets. This is the end of the job.

Click the blue Publish button at the top right. There is no confirmation step — it starts immediately, and a dialog tells you when it is done.

Publish starts the moment you click it

Unlike deleting, publishing asks you nothing first. Preview the procedure before you press it.

What publishing does

  • Sends every step's finished picture — background and annotations flattened into one image — to the cloud.
  • Sends the procedure itself, so the headsets can find it, with its alerts, tools and any video clips.
  • Numbers the steps from the order of the list.
Publishing again updates the same procedure

Found a mistake after publishing? Fix it and publish again. It updates the procedure that is already out there rather than creating a second copy — as long as you are working on the same procedure and have not started a new one in between.

Published pictures are readable by anyone with the link

This is how the headsets load them without signing in. Do not publish a step showing a password, a badge, or anything else you would not put on a noticeboard.

10Exporting a PDF

The other way to hand a procedure to somebody — for a binder, a noticeboard, or an operator who is not using a headset.

Choose File ▾ > Export PDF. The file downloads straight away, named after the procedure.

The PDF is a snapshot of the procedure as it stands right now. It has no connection to what is published — export a fresh one after you make changes.

11Saving and reopening

Step Builder holds one procedure at a time. Understanding where your work lives will save you losing an afternoon of it.

The automatic backup

Your work is saved continuously inside the browser as you go. Close the tab by accident and reopen the application, and the amber Resume editing bar on Manage Procedures brings it back.

The automatic backup is not a real backup

It lives in this browser, on this computer, and nowhere else. Clearing your browsing data erases it. It cannot be moved to another machine and a colleague cannot open it. For anything you would be upset to lose, save it properly.

The two places you can save

OptionWhat it does
SaveWrites back to wherever you last saved this procedure — a file or a cloud draft. If there is nowhere yet, it asks. The chip beside the button tells you what it will write to.
Save As to file…Choose a JSON file on your own machine. That file then becomes what Save writes to. Not shown in Safari or Firefox.
Save As to cloud…Save it as a cloud draft under a name you choose. This is what puts it on the Drafts tab, where you or a colleague can reopen it from another computer.
Open from file…Opens a procedure you saved to a file. Replaces what is currently open.
Open from cloud…Opens a cloud draft. Replaces what is currently open.
A cloud draft is not a published procedure

They are two different things in two different places. A draft is the editable version, and only people using this application see it. A published procedure is the flattened version the headsets read, and it cannot be opened back up for editing. Keep the draft if you ever want to change the procedure again.

Starting fresh

Orientation is permanent

The dialog says so: "Choose the canvas orientation. This can't be changed later." Choosing wrong means rebuilding the procedure from scratch. Importing a video picks the orientation for you automatically.

12Managing drafts and published procedures

Open Procedure ▾ > Manage Procedures… to see both sides of the work: what is still editable, and what is live on the headsets.

The difference between the tabs is what you can do with a row. A draft has Open and Delete. A published procedure has only Delete — it cannot be opened for editing. To change one, open its draft, or the file you saved, and publish again.

Deleting a procedure removes it from the headsets

The procedure and all its pictures are erased, and it disappears from the RealWear app. Operators looking for it will not find it. There is no undo. If you still have the draft you could publish it again — as a new procedure, with a new ID. Anyone who bookmarked the old one is out of luck.

Appendix C — Limits & gotchas

Browser

Use Chrome or Edge. In Safari and Firefox Save As to file… is not displayed at all.

Your draft

One procedure at a time. The automatic backup lives inside this browser only — clearing your browsing data erases it, it does not follow you to another computer, and colleagues cannot see it. Save As to cloud… is what makes it reachable from anywhere.

Four things replace your current work: New Procedure…, the two Import video options, and opening anything from a file or the cloud. Each warns you first, and the import and New Procedure dialogs offer Save JSON on the spot.

Video

MP4 (H.264) only, up to 2 GB. If a video is rejected, that is almost always the reason. Recording on an iPhone? Set Camera > Formats > Most Compatible and record again.

Pictures you add

Pictures are shrunk automatically when you add them, so the procedure stays fast to load on a headset. Very fine detail in a large image may soften — crop to the part that matters before adding it.

Video clips

Trimming happens in the cloud, so it needs a connection and takes a moment. Keep clips short: the headset downloads every clip in a procedure before it can be used offline.

Who did what

The name you type is a label, not a login. Anyone can type any name, and nothing verifies it. Use it for coordination, not for accountability.

Published pictures

Step pictures in a published procedure can be opened by anyone who has the direct link — this is how the headsets load them without signing in. Do not publish a step showing a password, a badge, or anything else you would not put on a noticeboard.

Things that cannot be undone

  • Deleting a recording permanently from Trash.
  • Deleting a published procedure in Manage Procedures.
  • Deleting a cloud draft in Manage Procedures.
  • The canvas orientation, once a procedure has been started.
